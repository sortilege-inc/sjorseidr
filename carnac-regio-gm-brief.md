# The Carnac Regio — GM Brief

*Ars Magica 5E / Definitive. Five nested levels, Magic-aligned. Discovered by Dagmar, Spring 1224. Playable 1224–1226.*

---

## 1. The Pitch

A five-level Magic regio beneath the drowned end of the Carnac alignments, entered through a tidal sea-cave. A sentinel at the mouth admits anyone who asks. A colony of degenerate merfolk holds the third level and has held it for a thousand years, tending a shrine to a king they have never seen. The fourth level is his throne room. The fifth is the road he will walk in on, and it opens on the night of the Great Conjunction of March 1226.

Nobody in the regio knows what the king is. Neither does the covenant.

**The inversion:** the party enters believing the goal is to reach level five. By the time they understand the site, the goal is to make sure it never opens — and the only people who know the building are a congregation who have prayed for exactly that opening since before the Order existed.

---

## 2. The Backstory Cascade

Roughly 1,000 years ago (c. 225 AD), **Jörmungandr shifted position.**

This was not an attack and not an event anyone witnessed. A body that encircles the world does not move locally. The consequences propagated outward as pressure:

1. **The serpent moves.** Ecosystems along the northern and western waters are disrupted.
2. **The fae migrate west,** displaced from their holdings, flooding into Atlantic waters that were not previously theirs.
3. **The merfolk are squeezed** out of the sea entirely and retreat *upward* into the Carnac regio, which was already ancient and already stacked.

None of the three tiers is aware of the tier above it. The merfolk blame the fae. The fae never met the serpent. The serpent noticed nothing.

**This is offstage backstory. It is not in the dungeon and cannot be resolved there.** Its function is to explain why the murfolk are on level 3 and why their grievance is permanently misdirected — the correction is a millennium away and out at sea.

### Table connections

- The fae moving **west** lands them in Irish waters. **Connacht bleeds in and out of faerie regiones; Éogan's fae attendants are the far end of this same cascade.** A player character is downstream of the backstory.
- The **sea-serpent skeleton at aura ~9** (Dagmar's lab site, where Éogan felt a tug from the depths) can now be read as a shed fragment, a rival that didn't move fast enough, or — if you want the covenant's home to become unbearable — not a skeleton at all.
- **Llewellyn's heresy** ("all magical regiones are ultimately one") gets a partial vindication he will hate: not a shared essence, a shared *disturbance*.
- **Erobos's methodological problem** — telling which observations belong to *a* regio versus *all* regiones — is answered. The observations that belong to all of them are the ones that belong to the serpent.

## 3. The Astronomy

### The Great Conjunction of 4–5 March 1226

Real event, and an exceptional one. Jupiter and Saturn closed to a separation of **2.1 arcminutes** — roughly a fifteenth of the moon's apparent width. It was the closest *observable* great conjunction between 1226 and 2020, and one of only two standout conjunctions in the catalogs across the whole span 1200–2400 CE. It occurred in **Aquarius, the Water-Bearer**, and it was an evening-sky event, not lost in solar glare.

To the naked eye the two heaviest planets fuse into a single new star.

**Why it works for this saga:**

- The lock on a maritime regio opens when the two greatest planets merge in a water sign. You did not have to invent that.
- 1224 → March 1226 is a **two-year in-fiction runway**. Survey, negotiation, the Paulus ward, Llewellyn's Zeno working — everything converges on a date the players can count down to.
- Medieval conjunctionist astrology treats Jupiter–Saturn conjunctions as the hinges of history. A magus with Artes Liberales and Philosophiae is reading standard literature, not inventing significance.
- **Dagmar's established method is premonitions plus astrology.** The discovery of the date is his to make, in character, without a handwave.

### Optional: the five-conjunction cluster

The astronomer A. Vibert Douglas identified **five separate planetary conjunctions across 1226–27** and argued they may explain why Genghis Khan abruptly broke off his campaign in China. If you want the number five doing extra work: five conjunctions, five levels, each level keyed to one, the fifth simply the last to arrive.

### Aldebaran and the Hyades

Carcosa lies under the Hyades, near Aldebaran.

- **Aldebaran is one of the fifteen Behenian fixed stars** of genuine medieval astrological magic — a royal star, a watcher. Period-legitimate; a magus can find it in real sources.
- **The Hyades are classically the "rainy stars,"** bringers of storms at sea. Maritime, which matches the covenant's entire regio grammar.

So the conjunction is the *visible* event; Aldebaran's position is what it points at. A sufficiently good astrologer works out that the alignment is not about Jupiter and Saturn at all — they are the hands of the clock, not the hour.

### Optional herald

**Halley's Comet passed in 1222**, two years before the discovery. Useful in retrospect as the thing Llewellyn saw and misread, or the event that woke the lower levels and produced the mirror-flash Dagmar caught in 1224.

## 4. Aura Structure and Tethers

Built with the RoP:Magic tether rules so the numbers are defensible rather than asserted.

**How tethers assemble:**

- Natural aura score = sum of all natural tethers, **capped at 5**
- Preternatural aura score = strongest preternatural tether, **+1 per additional tether**, capped at 5
- Base aura = natural + preternatural (ceiling 10)

**Relevant tether values:**

| Source | Score |
|---|---|
| Sea caves (natural) | 1–2 |
| Deep water / permanent immersion (natural) | 1–2 |
| Astrological alignment (natural — called out by name in the rules) | 1–2 |
| Megalithic monument (preternatural) | 1 per full century of *active use* |
| Magic being in residence ≥1 year (preternatural) | Might ÷ 10, rounded down |

**Tethers are per-level.** Each level of a regio has its own aura and therefore its own tethers. A tether operates on the level it physically occupies, not on the whole stack.

**Tormente, Might 40 → preternatural tether 4 — on level 1 only.** It is resident at the mouth. It contributes nothing to levels 2–5, which draw their auras from the monument and from natural features. Note also that this is a *tether*, not a flat +4 modifier: it sets a floor on level 1's preternatural side rather than stacking on top of everything. If Tormente leaves or dies, that tether drops a point — and level 1's aura with it, while the rest of the stack is unaffected.

**The alignments cap the preternatural side on the upper levels by themselves.** Menhirs and stone circles are explicitly listed as magic monuments at one point per century of active use. Carnac's stones plus a millennium of murfolk worship comfortably exceed 5. Add natural tethers (astrological alignment, water, caves, antiquity) and the natural side reaches 5 as well on the deepest levels.

**Base aura 10 at the top falls straight out of the rules. The gateway rating is not a fudge.**

### Worked example

| Level | Natural | Preternatural | Aura |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | caves 2 + water 1 = **3** | Tormente 4 = **4** | 7 |
| 2 | astrological alignment 2 + water 1 = **3** | monument (capped) **5** | 8 |
| 3 | + antiquity / permanent immersion = **4** | monument (capped) **5** | 9 |
| 4 | **5** | **5** | 10 |

Level 1 is the only level where a living creature is doing the work. That is worth knowing before the players start solving problems with violence.

### The ladder

| Level | Aura | EF to see the next level | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — The Cave | 7 | 7 (→8) | Hot. Tormente resident. |
| 2 — The Alignments | 8 | 9 (→9) | Sightline apparatus. |
| 3 — The Colony | 9 | 7 (→10) | Murfolk. Social gate. |
| 4 — The Throne Room | 10 | — | Empty. Waiting. |
| 5 — The Road | 10+ | Conjunction only | Not a room. |

*(Regio Sight Roll: Perception + appropriate Ability + stress die, against EF 5 + twice the aura difference. Second Sight, Magic Sensitivity, or InVi level 3 to detect boundaries — cast with a Vision target it gives the route between levels.)*

The mechanical wall and the social wall are the same wall: level 3 is the only floor from which level 4 can be reached, and the murfolk own it.

## 5. Level 1 — The Cave

**Aura 7. Magic. Tidal.**

A sea-cave at the drowned end of the alignments. Entry presents as a **mirror-flash** — a visual anomaly at a particular sun angle on a particular tide, undetectable at normal range. Carnac itself has missed it. This is consistent with the covenant's whole archive: every gateway they have recorded announced itself as a visual anomaly (Orkney's false second sun, the Carnac flash, shimmers like water without substance).

### The tide is the guardian

No creature required. The mouth only opens at a specific state of the tide and the cave floods. Every expedition is bounded: **in on the ebb, out on the flood, or not at all.**

This does three jobs — a guardian that cannot be fought, a clock on every session, and a daily-cycle rehearsal of the same logic that locks level 5 to March 1226. The regio teaches its own grammar on the ground floor.

### The reveal

**The alignments continue underwater.** (Er Lannic, the half-drowned stone circle in the Gulf of Morbihan, is real and does this work for you.) The stones do not stop at the shore. Whatever Carnac is, it was laid out when the water was somewhere else.

### Warping — the load-bearing rule

Auras of 6+ cause Warping, scaled by exposure. At **aura 7**:

| Exposure | Warping |
|---|---|
| Always within (live, work, sleep) | 1 / year |
| Half time within | 1 / 2 years |
| Frequent visits (~¼ of time) | **None** |

**Expeditions don't warp. Garrisons do.** Raiding the cave costs nothing — which is exactly why nobody notices it's dangerous. It costs when you moor a boat at the mouth, post a tide-watch, station someone to hold the entry open. Every sensible operational decision about Carnac is the one that hurts them.

**And the exemption that carries the theme:** characters with supernatural powers of the *same realm as the aura* — Hermetic magi in a Magic aura — **do not warp from it at all.**

Dagmar and Llewellyn can stand in that cave for years and feel nothing. The grogs cannot. The magi cannot perceive the harm they are doing to their own people, and the people bearing it have no vocabulary for it beyond feeling strange.

### What the warping looks like

RoP:Magic's guidance for a Water-tethered aura: inhabitants adapt to see through fog and stop minding the wet. Suggested Flaws are **Sensitive Sight**, a **Weakness for natural bodies of water**, and **Disfigured** — webbing between the digits, or skin that cracks and flakes unless kept wet. A suggested Virtue is **Great Immunity: Drowning**. An Aquam-aligned aura adds: skin cracks if not kept wet, and at Warping Score 8+ a character may begin to *move by flowing*.

This is what happens to **the covenant's own people** — the boat crew, the tide-watch, anyone stationed at the mouth. Visibly, inside two years, in ways the other grogs will gossip about long before any magus notices.

Also note, from the aligned-aura rules: at high levels *inhabitants become so adjusted to the demands of the place that they find it very hard to leave.*

### The misreading (optional, and it is a mistake)

The murfolk are **not** warped humans (see §7). But a magus who sees a webbed, water-adapted grog on level 1 and a colony of webbed, water-adapted beings on level 3 will very plausibly draw the obvious line between them.

It is false. Convergent adaptation to the same aura, not shared ancestry. But it is a conclusion the party can reach honestly, act on, and be wrong about — and it will insult the murfolk badly if voiced, because they know exactly what they are and have never been anything else.

### Mundane hazard, stacked underneath

Cold, depth, drowning, a tide that does not negotiate. Fatigue and drowning rules; no supernatural mechanism needed. But the aura makes everything *more* — colors sharper, sounds more piercing — so the cold is more cold, and men come out shaking and elated and wanting to go back.

**Two hazards, two clocks.** The tide can kill you today. The aura will change you over years. The party will only ever plan for the first.

### Tormente

Might 40. Stationed at the mouth. **It is not there to guard what comes in.**

It will let the party through, cheerfully. Tell them the tide. Name the stones. Warn them about the cold. It will say nothing useful about coming back out, because that is not the brief.

The brief is that **nothing else comes out.**

Play it as a *post*, not a monster — a sentinel that has held a station for centuries with no relief and no orders, which is a far stranger thing to talk to. Who set it? Does it still report to anyone? It may not know.

**Implications:**

- A Might 40 guardian implies the thing being contained outranks it. Players can estimate what is upstairs by looking at what is parked downstairs. The estimate should frighten them.
- The mirror-flash being undetectable at range is not the regio being subtle. **That is containment working.**
- The party can bring anything down — supplies, a lab, more magi, an army. Tormente holds the door. They cannot take anything back.
- **Mid-campaign beat:** the first time a grog who has been changing tries to leave, and Tormente does not move aside, because by whatever standard it applies he has stopped counting as something that came in.
- **The lever the players will find:** killing Tormente drops the tether and cools the cave from 7 toward 3, making level 1 safe to garrison. It does *nothing* to levels 2–5, which are held up by the monument. So the trade is exact and legible: they can protect their own people, at the cost of opening the mouth of Carnac to everything else and removing the only thing keeping the contents in. The deep levels stay just as lethal either way.

## 6. Level 2 — The Alignments

**Aura 8. The puzzle room.**

Stones continuing under the water, standing whole. This is the apparatus that opens level 5, and **it is operated from level 2 but acts on level 5** — which makes the dungeon non-linear. The party must fight up, learn what the lock is, and come back down to set it. Level 3 gets crossed twice, in both directions, with the murfolk's disposition possibly changed in between.

### The sightline mechanism

Megalithic alignments work by foresight and backsight — a near stone and a far stone that merge from exactly one standing place.

**The pillars must be arranged so that, viewed from the entrance to level 3, they reproduce the March 1226 conjunction.** Seven pillars for the seven planets (Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon), with Aldebaran as the fixed anchor they are measured against.

Two pillars do not need to share a socket. They need to be at different **distances**, so that from the door they overlap. Distance encodes the arcminute separation.

### Why this is the right design

- **The vantage point is the murfolk's front door.** The apparatus cannot be surveyed, tested, or verified from anywhere else. You cannot work on it without standing on their threshold, repeatedly, for as long as it takes. The social gate and the mechanical gate become the same problem.
- **Parallax makes it a species question.** The alignment is only true at one eye height. A configuration set correctly by murfolk reckoning is subtly wrong for a standing human, and vice versa. *Whoever the stones were set for is recoverable from the geometry — and it is not the murfolk.*
- **It forces split parties and shouted corrections.** One person at the door calling; others out among the stones moving them blind. Nobody moving a pillar can see whether they have helped. And someone is standing in the murfolk's doorway the entire time.
- **It matches the covenant's gateway signature.** Two stones becoming one stone is exactly the idiom of the false second sun and the mirror-flash. When it finally aligns, what appears at the end of the sightline is the same phenomenon Nequam saw off Orkney. The archive has been describing this mechanism for six years without knowing it.

### There is no saboteur

Nobody has ever solved this. The apparatus has stood mis-set since it was raised, not because anyone hid it, but because no one who could read the sky has ever stood in that doorway. **The murfolk's ignorance is the only thing that has ever kept the door shut.**

### The complication

The murfolk have looked at this arrangement every day for forty generations. It is their skyline. They will notice a moved stone before the party finishes moving it, and they will have opinions about which arrangement is *correct* — because somewhere in a thousand years, someone down there worked out that the stones were *for* something, without ever learning what.

So the murfolk may not be an obstacle at this stage. **They may be eager.** They have been waiting for someone who could read the sky, and the magi are visibly qualified.

### Other level-2 material

- **First yellow.** Not a sign, not a symbol. RoP:Magic gives you *vestiges* — semi-illusory objects and images that fill Magic Realm locations. A tattered awning over nothing. A figure at the edge of the alignments in a color that should not survive underwater. Something staged. Nobody should be able to say afterward whether they actually saw it.
- **The offerings.** A path of them, small, salt-crusted, unmistakably made by hands, all facing up. This is the thread to level 3, and it tells the players there is a congregation before they meet it.
- **Optional: the counting.** Carnac folklore holds the stones cannot be counted twice the same. Use it as a lock (get the number right to ascend), a census (the number has been dropping, slowly, for a thousand years — nobody works out what is being counted until level 4), or a tempus (the stones are being raised on loop by people who cannot see you; watch long enough to learn the number, too long and you are in the loop).

## 7. Level 3 — The Colony

**Aura 9. The murfolk. The negotiation.**

### Register

**Murlocs, not mermaids.** Croaking, wide-mouthed, bulge-eyed, wattled. Salvage as carapace — they wear ship-timber the way the hermit crab at the Scandinavian sea-mount wore half a hull, which is an established motif in the covenant's own archive.

This register is correct for Magic-aligned beings, who are supposed to be unsettling and indifferent rather than charming.

### What they are

**A distinct magical species.** Not warped humans, not transformed anything — a separate branch, Magic-aligned from origin, with their own essential nature. Their anatomy is theirs. They were webbed and wide-mouthed and wattled when they still had the open sea, and they will be after. Give them Magic Might and treat them as a people, not as a condition.

This matters for how they read at the table. They are not a cautionary tale about what the regio does to you. They are the natives, and the party are the strange-looking ones.

Refugees, third-order, from the serpent cascade. They fled the fae; the fae fled the serpent; they never saw the serpent.

They do not have a history. They have a **grievance**, polished for a thousand years into liturgy. They chant it. They have it wrong.

### What they believe

There is a king on the floor above. He took them in when the fae drove them from the water. They tend his threshold, leave offerings, and have done so for forty generations. He will rise when they are finally owed.

They are correct that he will rise. Everything else is wrong.

### The prison, not the sanctuary

Tormente does not let things *out*. The murfolk have been inside a sealed vessel for a thousand years, with a guardian at the door that will not release them and does not consider them the point.

Forty generations of that produces something worse than a grievance: a theology in which the sea they remember is the promised land, and the king upstairs is the one who will open the way.

They are right that the door is shut. They are wrong about who shut it, and why.

**So they do not want help going up. They want help getting out.** Level 3 is a prison bargain, and the party is the first thing in ten centuries to walk in the front door with any prospect of walking back out.

### Running the negotiation

The party must cross level 3 **twice** — up, and back down to set the pillars. Their standing can change between crossings.

The thing they must not say is the one thing that would break the murfolk entirely. Whether the party works out what is upstairs *before* or *after* they need murfolk cooperation is the single biggest pacing decision in the adventure.

## 8. Level 4 — The Throne Room

**Aura 10. Empty. Waiting.**

A throne room for a king. The murfolk think it is theirs.

It is **Hastur's**.

### Realm assignment — the key design decision

**Do not file him under Infernal.** The Infernal in Ars Magica means demons: a defined theology, a chain of command, temptation as method. Putting the King in Yellow there flattens him into a duke of Hell in a mask, and worse, makes him *legible* — the party would know what they were dealing with.

**Put him in the Twilight Void.**

RoP:Magic describes the Void as the second part of the Magic Realm: sparse, dark, foreboding, full of omens and portents, home to ancient and powerful beings it is wisest not to disturb. Cosm theorists call it the space between cosms; the historical school calls it the time between times. It is, in the published text, where the things too old and too wrong for the rest of the Realm live — **and the rules give you no bestiary for it.**

That is Hastur's address, and claiming it costs you nothing.

**Two benefits:**

1. **It stays Magic-aligned**, so the magi are not eating double botch dice in the throne room. (DE clarifies the 5E wording: doubled botch dice apply in a regio whose *realm differs from that of the power*, not merely any regio you don't live in.) This matters if anyone plans to fight there.
2. **It gives you a delivery vector requiring no travel at all.** Magi who go into Twilight go into the Void. A bad botch, a Warping-heavy season, and someone meets him without ever leaving the lab. That horror is free from the system.

### What is actually in the room

Nothing. An absence with the shape of an occupant. A seat, maintained, running on habit, tended by a congregation who have not been told anything.

The mark on the floor is not fae. That is where the murfolk's liturgy fails.

### The Yellow Sign as regio path

DE explicitly allows entry by following a particular path or ritual — sometimes sufficient by itself, sometimes a precondition for any roll at all, sometimes available only on a particular day or to a particular kind of person. The Yellow Sign fits that slot exactly.

**And it propagates.** Anyone who learns it can find the way. The moment the party writes it down, sketches it in a lab text, or describes it to a Redcap, the regio acquires a new door. **Their own scholarship is the vector** — a very Ars Magica failure mode.

## 9. Level 5 — The Road

Not a room. The far side.

RoP:Magic notes that regio gateways into the Magic Realm proper are always guarded by fierce creatures on the far side, and that they typically open **inward only**. Insulae are theorized to be Magic regiones with auras exceeding 10. Aura 10 already reads in the core rules as a gateway to the realm of its affiliation.

The conjunction does not unlock a chamber. It makes the crossing survivable, for one night.

**Which inverts the dungeon.** The goal is not to reach level 5. It is to be standing in the throne room in March 1226 and prevent the stairs from appearing — while the only people who know the building are convinced the party has come to rob their church.

## 10. The Three Theories (Llewellyn's Ammunition)

RoP:Magic gives three competing scholarly models of the Magic Realm, none provable:

- **Cosms** — worlds contained within single things; a forest inside every tree, a country inside a pebble. The Twilight Void is the space *between* cosms.
- **The Magic Archipelago** — insulae, islands in a great sea, navigated by "Ferrymen." The Void is the deep water between islands.
- **Tempora** — each location is a moment in history; deeper means older. Popular with Criamon. The Void is the time between times.

**Orkney was already a cosm reading** — room-sized spherical microcosms hanging in liquid sky. Carnac in the archipelago idiom is the *same phenomenon under a different theory*.

That is exactly the ammunition Llewellyn needs and exactly the trap Erobos warned about: two magi, two models, one regio, incompatible maps. The published text is explicit that a group of magi devoted to sustained exploration *might* make one theory harder to sustain — and that if so, the storyguide decides the underlying truth. That is a campaign-length prize, and it is on the table.

## 11. Open Decisions

1. **Is it actually Jörmungandr, or a faerie wearing the story?** ArM5 takes this seriously — Magic beings belong to the natural order and are indifferent to humans; faeries are made of the stories humans tell. A world-serpent that displaces ecosystems without noticing is impeccably Magic-aligned. But a faerie inhabiting the Midgard Serpent role produces near-identical evidence, and RoP:Faerie is explicit that people in Mythic Europe do not reliably tell them apart. Live dispute for Erobos and Llewellyn — with a mechanical edge, since realm assignment determines botch dice.
2. **How cosmic is Hastur allowed to be?** In Mythic Europe the Divine is objectively real and outranks everything, so he cannot be a genuinely unopposable cosmic force. Treat that as the *solution*: it makes the horror local, survivable at ruinous cost, and resistible by the Church in ways the magi will hate needing. The alternative — the Void predates the ordering of the realms and what is in it was never sorted — buys the full nihilist register but puts you in an argument with the setting's metaphysics on a night you would rather be running a fight.
3. **Does the covenant's sea-serpent skeleton stay a skeleton?**
4. **Will someone say Ragnarök?** The 1226–27 conjunction cluster was historically read as an omen of exactly that magnitude. Whether that reading is correct is the largest lever in the brief.

### Edition note

Regio mechanics are identical in 5E and Definitive. DE restores detail 5E compressed: a fuller treatment of path-and-ritual entry, an explicit statement that the Ease Factor can swing by large amounts on particular days or through particular activities, a note that some regiones are effectively unenterable, and the observation that a regio enterable only by being led is the most secure covenant site there is. DE also disambiguates the "foreign regio" botch-dice clause to mean a regio of a *different realm from the power*.

Since Magic auras (unlike faerie ones) do not fluctuate cyclically, **hang the March 1226 lock on the path, not the aura.** The aura stays constant at 10; only the stairs are conditional. DE's Ease Factor language covers this explicitly.
