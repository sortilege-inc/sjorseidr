How vis sources are classified

Not every source on this page belongs to the Tribunal — a covenant's own Seisins and Legacies aren't listed here at all. Only the shared, Tribunal-owned categories below are.

Seisin
A vis source a covenant can walk to, retrieve, and return home with within a day — theirs by right, no Tourney involved.
Legacy
A source that doesn't qualify as a seisin but which the Tribunal has grandfathered in to a covenant anyway. Newly discovered sources count as legacies until the next Tribunal, when the status is either renewed or the source becomes a tropaeum.
Tropaeum
Tribunal common property, requiring minimal effort to harvest, distributed on seven-year leases through the Hermetic Tourney. The leaseholder has exclusive rights for the term.
Luctatio
Tribunal common property that anyone may attempt — no lease, no prior claim, effort or danger required. Having harvested it before grants no privilege on future attempts.

Tourney winners may also take volumes from the Tribunal library; those volumes, and any copies made of them, remain Tribunal property. Raiding another covenant's non-magical goods is, per Normandy custom, technically legal.

Tropaea Tribunal common property — won by Tourney lease

Vis sources requiring minimal effort to harvest. Distributed among Tribunal members on seven-year leases through the Hermetic Tourney. The holder of a lease has exclusive rights to harvest.

Luctationes Tribunal common property — open to all, effort or danger required

Vis sources requiring significant effort, skill, or danger to harvest. Any magus may attempt them, but success confers no exclusive rights in subsequent years. Winning a luctatio is a feat in itself.

Annual Canon
A tree in a forest has a basilisk family nesting in its roots. The fruit of the tree, if the basilisks permit collection, is Perdo vis.

Annual Canon
The mournful song of a ghostly maiden is Mentem vis. She must first be enticed to sing her lament, then the magus must capture the song in physical form.

Annual Canon
A falcon nests on a cliff ledge every year. On the same day, a serpent tries to steal an egg. If prevented, the egg contains Animal vis. If the serpent succeeds, it contains Vim vis (if caught).

Animal
Variable Proposed
A siren inhabits the sea-cave near St. Malo. If persuaded to leave the cave willingly — not tricked or compelled — scales she sheds passing into daylight are Animal vis. She shatters boats in response to deception.
The Ghost Fleet of Barfleur
Vim
Annual (winter) Proposed
On stormy winter nights, a spectral fleet passes Barfleur. The trailing ship's lantern still burns. A magus who boards it by ordinary seamanship and asks the captain where he sails receives a coal that is Vim vis.
The Faux de Verzy
Perdo
Variable Proposed
The dark dwarfs of the twisted beech grove leave behind bundles of Perdo vis — remnants of their enchantments. Must be collected at night. Faerie aura 4; Hermetic magic is unreliable and draws their attention.
The Boar King's Hunt
Animal
Annual (autumn) Proposed
Finding one of the Boar King's abandoned carcasses and reporting it to his court before the ravens do earns the right to harvest — the heart contains Animal vis. Taking without reporting is a grave insult he never forgets.
The Hague-Dicke Ghost
Mentem
Annual (winter solstice) Proposed
An old Norse ghost recites the names of all he killed on the longest night. Listen through to dawn without speaking, and he says one true thing about your future. Written in Old Norse immediately, this is Mentem vis. Any interruption rouses all the ghosts.
Kerbourg Megalith Alley
Rego
Annual (equinox) Proposed
Two consenting magi perform certamen within the alley on the equinox. The loser's sigil pools in the capstone. Marsh-water held up to catch the drip holds Rego vis. The stones know if certamen is faked.
The Lutin's Sacred Fire
Ignem
Annual (winter) Proposed
Lutin servants of a Norman farmstead light a fire without wood in an old sacred well each winter. If a magus earns a coal from them freely — not by trade, compulsion, or magic — the coal is Ignem vis and stays lit for a year.
The Shifting Book, Abbaye de Jumièges
Muto
Annual Proposed
Recovered "pages" (actually fish-skin) whose text shifts over weeks. A scholar who reads cover-to-cover and recites the changed passages from memory absorbs Muto vis, manifest as iridescence in their saliva for one season. The monks believe it a holy relic.
Ruins of Caput Capra, Loire
Perdo
Annual Proposed
On the anniversary of the youngest Diedne covenant's fall, a fist-sized lump of fused stone and ash appears in the ruin. Always in a different location. Confluensis apprentices visit annually — harvesting in their presence is diplomatically fraught.
The Branugurix Seal, Mount Dol
Vim
1 pawn/year (if successful) Proposed — High Danger
The sealed entrance to the Diedne domus magna thins once yearly. Pressing a hand against the hillside and resisting the seal's pushback (Stamina + Magic Theory 12+) draws out Vim vis. Botching risks being pulled inside. House Guernicus actively watches this site.

Notable Opponents 1223 Hermetic Tourney — GM prep, cross-checked against the actual bracket

Sjórseiðr's Certamen ended in the first round; Dimicatio and Hastiludium both carried them all the way to a semifinal/final that the GM's own bracket sheet never filled in (see the Storyteller page's open question on this). Opponents below are marked accordingly — this is prep material, not a claim that every round was played out in full.

Certamen — 25/15/8/8 points, House Flambeau's mental-conflict duel

The PC's draw was brutal on paper (Fudarus → Confluensis → Bellum Iocari → Merces Aurea), but the actual bracket has Sjórseiðr losing round one — so only the first opponent was ever faced. The other three are preserved here as unused prep.

Myrrha of Fudarus Round 1 — Sjórseiðr lost
House Tytalus · Domus Magna, founded 798 · Mentem 9, Creo 5, Intellego 5, Rego 5 · Great Intelligence · ~19–21 base certamen total

“Your parens did you a disservice, teaching you comfort before teaching you struggle. I will remedy that.”

Predatory calm, unsettling Piercing Gaze, barely speaks before a duel. Wincing and teeth-grinding betray her Painful Magic flaw with every spell. Fudarus was humiliated by Scarem Montem's dimicatio win, and Myrrha carries that shame — desperate to redeem her covenant. Picks Intellego or Rego as Technique to force the fight into her Mentem 9; will burn Fatigue via Life Boost and save a Confidence point for the critical exchange.

Proctor of Confluensis Unused — Sjórseiðr never reached round 2
House Guernicus · Intellego 15 (Affinity + Puissant), Mentem 6, Imaginem 6, Corpus 5 · Great Perception, Curse of Venus · ~24–27 base

“The records of Confluensis go back four centuries. I have studied every certamen in our archives. I know how this ends.”

Treats certamen as a legal proceeding — recites rules with pedantic exactness. His Side Effect virtue means lying causes visible discomfort, so he can't bluff. Curse of Venus makes him socially awkward despite Hermetic Prestige. Wins on attrition with Intellego 15; his Forms (5–6) are the opening, if a challenger can force one.

Tacticus of Bellum Iocari Unused — Sjórseiðr never reached the semifinal
House Tremere · "War Games" specialists, founded 1185 · all Techniques 5, Ignem/Auram/Terram 9, Aquam 8 · Minor Magical Focus (Certamen) · ~26 base

“Florum thought their centuries of experience mattered more than preparation. They were wrong. Now — shall we?”

Just upset Florum, one of the Five Great Lieges, and is riding the momentum — young, ambitious, and genuinely enjoying himself. His Minor Magical Focus adds his lower Art again to any elemental-Form total, which is devastating; his Mentem is only 1, the one clean opening.

Aurelian of Merces Aurea Unused — the final Sjórseiðr never reached
House Verditius · Lotharingian separatist leader, founded 1175 · Terram 15 (Affinity + Puissant), Creo 7, Muto 5, Rego 5 · Faerie Blood (Dwarf) · ~27–29 base

“The Five Lieges have ruled by tradition, not merit. I am here to demonstrate the difference.”

Already crushed the other half of the bracket, decisively beating Cunfin in his side's final. Every win is propaganda for the separatist cause — his Dwarf flaw and Faerie Blood are visible and he doesn't care. Terram 15 makes him near-unbeatable on his own ground; outside it, Mentem and Imaginem are both 0.

Dimicatio — 23/12/6/6 points, House Flambeau's forceless-casting duel

Sjórseiðr won three straight rounds here, reaching a semifinal against Nidi that the GM's bracket sheet leaves blank (see the Storyteller page). All four opponents below were live prep, not discarded brainstorm.

Exspectatio Round 1 — Sjórseiðr won
Vassal of Fudarus, founded 1201, Brittany — Tytalus apprentice-level, still proving themselves to their lieges

“Fudarus expects results. We will not disappoint them.” [nervous swallow]

Likely champion Cadoc (young, overconfident) or Herve (a hastiludium specialist fighting to redeem an earlier decisive loss). The name means "anticipation" — upstarts waiting for their chance.

Cunfin Later round — Sjórseiðr won
Vassal of Confluensis (Guernicus), founded 1192, Champagne & Burgundy — methodical, disciplined

“The law is merely the framework within which conflict is permitted. Shall we test the boundaries?”

Champion Reynard, a Tytalus magus serving a Guernicus vassal — notable enough on its own. Won their own sudden-death certamen tiebreaker against Exspectatio, suggesting quick thinking under pressure.

Turris Vigilans Later round — Sjórseiðr won
"Watchtower," independent, founded 1170 — defensive specialists

“We have watched the Tribunal for fifty years. We have seen every trick. Show us something new.”

Champion Vigilus (former Quaesitor) or Sentinus (Tremere ward specialist). Expect excellent fast-cast defenses from a covenant built around watching and waiting.

Nidi Semifinal — result unresolved (q021)
Vassal of Oleron

Oleron themselves were dominating the other half of this bracket via Berenguer de Tolosa (Flambeau, dimicatio master) — "beautiful, fluid southern French style." Nidi, as Oleron's own vassal, may have faced their liege in the semifinal on the other side of the draw. Berenguer: "In Provence, we call this art. In Normandy, you call it combat. We shall see which interpretation prevails."

Hastiludium — 20/10/5/5 points, team event on mundane "mounts"

Three magi per side, balanced on a peasant-village item (barrel, cauldron, pig — no longer than a broom, no wider than a beer barrel), using magic to propel and unseat. Touch ground, you're out. Sjórseiðr had a natural edge here as sailors with sea-legs, and won three straight before the same semifinal gap hits (q021).

Montverte (hosts) Round 1 — Sjórseiðr won
Praeco Guillaume le Preux — hosting while their own Tremere vassal (Scarem Montem) publicly rebels

“We have hosted this Tribunal with honor. Now we shall defend it with valor. The heirs of William the Conqueror do not yield the field.”

Montverte could not afford to lose another event, having already dropped a dimicatio tiebreaker to Semita Errabunda — desperate, possibly mistake-prone, but also drawing on a strong Norman mounted-combat tradition as hosts. See t032 for the unresolved alliance-offer thread this opponent seeds.

Arx Argentea Later round — Sjórseiðr won
"Silver Tower," independent, founded 1188 — Verditius metal-crafting, Criamon moon/silver mysteries

Fielded Argentius (silver crafting) and Ferrus (metal specialist) alongside Lunara (Criamon). Expect metal manipulation — silver or iron mounts, exceptional Terram/metal magic on both offense and defense.

Eboris Later round — Sjórseiðr won
"Ivory," Paris, founded 1204 — status unclear, fighting mad

Lost their own certamen match to Requies Aeterna on a penalty (Maurice's "creative interpretation" of seniority rules) and were demanding a formal hearing. Fulbert is furious. Expect extra aggression from a covenant that feels cheated.

Bellum Iocari / Lapis Crudus Semifinal & final — result unresolved (q021)
Lapis Crudus won their side's final against Bellum Iocari — 160 years in a faerie regio, "antique techniques"

Champion Petronius (Merinita) uses pre-Schism War strategies that predate the current Peripheral Code — technically legal, deeply unsettling to face.

GM prep notes carried over from the tourney planning
  • Veto rules: the older magus picks Technique, the younger picks Form; each may veto one of the opponent's best Arts.
  • Dimicatio is forceless casting (Penetration 0) — first spell to reach the opponent's Parma wins. Fast-cast defense is at −10, and needs to match half the spell's level to block.
  • Half the crowd was rooting for Sjórseiðr as romantic underdogs; maritime magi have a reputation for pragmatism.
  • The Cabal of the Golden Coin — a mysterious team consistently placing 14th — may have approached the party with an offer to fix a contest for a vis bribe. Unconfirmed whether this was ever actually pitched at the table.
